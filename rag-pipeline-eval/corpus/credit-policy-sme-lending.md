# Credit Policy: SME Lending

Definition. A small or medium enterprise (SME) is a business group with annual turnover
below AUD 50 million. Facilities include overdrafts, term loans, equipment finance and
trade finance.

Delegated limits. The maximum aggregate exposure to one SME group under delegated
authority is AUD 5 million. Exposures above AUD 5 million must be approved by the Credit
Committee, and exposures above AUD 20 million by the Board Credit Committee.

Financial covenants. New facilities require a minimum debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)
of 1.25 times and an interest cover ratio of at least 2.0 times, tested on the most recent
financial year. Covenants are tested quarterly for facilities above AUD 1 million and
annually otherwise.

Information requirements. Applicants must provide two years of financial statements and
the most recent twelve months of business bank statements. Start-up businesses (less than
two years trading) are eligible only for facilities fully secured by residential property
or cash.

Concentration limits. No single industry may exceed 15% of the total SME portfolio, and
construction lending is capped at 10% of the SME portfolio. Limits are monitored monthly
by Portfolio Risk.

Reviews. Every SME facility is subject to an annual review including updated financials,
security valuation status and covenant compliance. Facilities on the watchlist are reviewed
quarterly.

Pricing. Risk-based pricing uses the internal SME rating grade (1 to 10); grades 8 to 10
are non-standard and require Credit Risk sign-off on pricing and structure.
