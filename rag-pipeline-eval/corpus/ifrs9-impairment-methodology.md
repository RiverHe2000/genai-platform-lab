# IFRS 9 Impairment Methodology

Staging. Financial assets are allocated to three stages. Stage 1 assets carry a
12-month expected credit loss (ECL). Stage 2 assets, which have experienced a significant
increase in credit risk (SICR) since origination, carry a lifetime ECL. Stage 3 assets are
credit-impaired and also carry a lifetime ECL with interest recognised on the net carrying
amount.

Significant increase in credit risk. An exposure is transferred to Stage 2 when any of the
following applies: (a) the account is 30 days past due (the backstop); (b) the lifetime
probability of default (PD) has at least doubled since origination and increased by at
least 50 basis points in absolute terms; (c) the exposure is placed on the credit
watchlist; or (d) the exposure has been granted forbearance.

Default. An exposure is in default, and therefore Stage 3, when it is 90 days past due or
when the bank judges the borrower unlikely to pay without realising security.

Measurement. ECL is calculated as the product of the probability of default (PD), the loss
given default (LGD) and the exposure at default (EAD), discounted at the effective interest
rate. Lifetime PD term structures are derived from 12-month PDs using a Markov transition
matrix conditioned on the macroeconomic scenario.

Scenarios. Three forward-looking scenarios are probability-weighted: base case 50%,
downside 30% and upside 20%. A severe downside overlay is applied when forecast
unemployment exceeds 8%.

Cure. An exposure exits Stage 3 after 12 consecutive months of performance under the
contractual terms. An exposure exits Stage 2 after a 6-month probation period in which
none of the SICR triggers applies.

Governance. The methodology is owned by Finance, and the PD, LGD and EAD models are Tier 1
models under the Model Risk Management Policy.
